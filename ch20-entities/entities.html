Chapter 20 - HTML Entities

Understanding HTML Entities

HTML entities are special codes used to represent characters that have special meaning in HTML or are difficult to type directly. They begin with an ampersand (&) and end with a semicolon (;), allowing you to display characters that might otherwise be interpreted as HTML code or aren't available on your keyboard.

Entity Types

  • Named entities - Use descriptive names (e.g., &lt; for <)
  • Decimal entities - Use decimal numbers (e.g., &#60; for <)
  • Hexadecimal entities - Use hex numbers (e.g., &#x3C; for <)

Common Reserved Characters

< - &lt; or &#60; > - &gt; or &#62; & - &amp; or &#38; " - &quot; or &#34; ' - &apos; or &#39;

Special Characters

© - &copy; or &#169; ® - &reg; or &#174; ™ - &trade; or &#8482; € - &euro; or &#8364; £ - &pound; or &#163; ¥ - &yen; or &#165; ° - &deg; or &#176; ± - &plusmn; or &#177; × - &times; or &#215; ÷ - &divide; or &#247;

Space Entities

Non-breaking space - &nbsp; or &#160; En space - &ensp; or &#8194; Em space - &emsp; or &#8195; Thin space - &thinsp; or &#8201;

Mathematical Symbols

∀ - &forall; or &#8704; ∂ - &part; or &#8706; ∃ - &exist; or &#8707; ∅ - &empty; or &#8709; ∇ - &nabla; or &#8711; ∈ - &isin; or &#8712; ∉ - &notin; or &#8713; ∋ - &ni; or &#8715; ∏ - &prod; or &#8719; ∑ - &sum; or &#8721;

Currency Symbols

$ - &#36; ¢ - &cent; or &#162; £ - &pound; or &#163; ¤ - &curren; or &#164; ¥ - &yen; or &#165; € - &euro; or &#8364;

Arrows

← - &larr; or &#8592; ↑ - &uarr; or &#8593; → - &rarr; or &#8594; ↓ - &darr; or &#8595; ↔ - &harr; or &#8596; ⇐ - &lArr; or &#8656; ⇒ - &rArr; or &#8658; ⇔ - &hArr; or &#8660;

Best Practices

  • Use named entities - More readable and maintainable
  • Declare character encoding - Use UTF-8 when possible
  • Be consistent - Stick to one entity style
  • Consider compatibility - Check browser support
  • Document usage - Comment complex entities
  • Validate output - Ensure correct rendering

Common Use Cases

  • Displaying code examples
  • Copyright notices
  • Mathematical formulas
  • Special typography
  • Multi-language content
  • Legal documents

Accessibility Considerations

  • Use semantic HTML when possible
  • Ensure screen readers can interpret entities
  • Provide context for special characters
  • Test with different assistive technologies
  • Consider alternative text representations
