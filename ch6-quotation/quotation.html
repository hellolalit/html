Chapter 6 - Quotations and Citations in HTML

Understanding Quotations

HTML provides several elements for marking up quotations and citations. These elements help maintain proper semantic structure and provide appropriate styling for quoted content.

Inline Quotations (Q Tag)

<p>As Shakespeare wrote, <q>All the world's a stage</q></p>

Example:

The famous quote Be the change you wish to see in the world is often attributed to Gandhi.

Block Quotations (BLOCKQUOTE Tag)

<blockquote cite="URL">
  A longer quotation that typically spans
  multiple lines and is displayed as a block.
</blockquote>

Example:

The only way to do great work is to love what you do. If you haven't found it yet, keep looking. Don't settle. As with all matters of the heart, you'll know when you find it.
— Steve Jobs

Citations (CITE Tag)

<p>My favorite book is <cite>The Hobbit</cite> by J.R.R. Tolkien.</p>

Example:

The article The Future of Web Development discusses modern trends in web technologies.

Best Practices

  • Use q for short quotes: Inline quotes that are part of a paragraph
  • Use blockquote for longer quotes: Standalone quotes that deserve their own block
  • Include citations: Use the cite attribute to reference sources
  • Style appropriately: Use CSS to enhance the presentation of quotes
  • Maintain context: Ensure quotes make sense in their surrounding content

Styling Quotations

blockquote {
  margin: 1em 2em;
  padding: 1em;
  border-left: 4px solid #ccc;
  background: #f9f9f9;
}

q {
  font-style: italic;
  color: #666;
}

Common Use Cases

  1. Academic papers and research documents
  2. Blog posts and articles
  3. Testimonials and reviews
  4. Literary analysis
  5. News articles and journalism
